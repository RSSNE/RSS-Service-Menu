Section 1: What is the NIHR Research Support Service?

The NIHR Research Support Service (RSS) is a free, expert service that helps researchers — including companies like yours — design and deliver high-quality health and care research. We work with people developing products, technologies, services, and care models that have the potential to improve outcomes for patients and the public.

We are not a funding body. We do not award grants. What we do is help you build the strongest possible research evidence base, so that when you apply for NIHR funding or other investment, your proposal is rigorous, credible, and competitive.

In short: we help you ask the right research question, in the right way, with the right evidence, before you submit your funding application.

Who delivers the RSS?

The RSS is delivered through eight regional Hubs and a set of national Specialist Centres. Each Hub brings together leading universities, research organisations, and NHS partners — giving you access to local support with national expertise. The RSS is funded by the National Institute for Health and Care Research (NIHR), part of the Department of Health and Social Care.

Why does the RSS work with companies?

Health and care innovation increasingly comes from companies — particularly SMEs developing diagnostics, medical devices, digital health tools, therapeutics, and social care technologies. Independent, rigorous research evidence is what NHS commissioners, NICE, and procurement decision-makers require before adopting new technologies. We help you generate that evidence.

Is the service really free?

Yes. RSS support is funded by NIHR and provided at no cost to you. You do not need to be an existing NIHR grant holder, and there is no obligation to apply for NIHR funding as a result of working with us — though many companies do.

Section 2: Are You Eligible? — The Three Checks

Before we can offer substantive research support, we confirm that your company and innovation meet NIHR's eligibility requirements. If you do not yet qualify, we will point you to the right support for your stage (see Appendix A).

Check 1: Are you an eligible company?

Your organisation must be registered as a limited company, community interest company (CIC), or equivalent with Companies House.

SME size thresholds used by NIHR
Company sizeEmployeesAnnual turnoverEligible for NIHR RSS?
MicroFewer than 10Up to €2 millionYes
Small10–49Up to €10 millionYes
Medium50–249Up to €50 millionYes
Large250 or moreOver €50 millionNo — not eligible as an SME

Charity, CIC, or social enterprise? Eligible if registered as a limited company or CIC with Companies House and within size thresholds.

Sole trader or partnership? Not currently eligible as a lead organisation. Registering as a limited company is straightforward via Companies House.

Check your status: find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk — confirm 'Active' status and company type (Ltd, PLC, or CIC).

Check 2: Is your technology at the right stage? (TRL3 minimum)

NIHR support is for innovations beyond pure concept — there must be at least some experimental or feasibility evidence. This is Technology Readiness Level 3 (TRL3), or 'proof of concept'. If below TRL3, we signpost you to routes such as Innovate UK feasibility funding. See Section 7 for a plain-language TRL explainer.

Check 3: Is your research question within NIHR scope?

The RSS supports research into effectiveness, safety, feasibility, and implementation of health and care interventions — including clinical trials, real-world evaluations, feasibility studies, health economics, and patient and public involvement. We do not cover basic laboratory science, commercial market research, or business development.

Section 3: What We Can — and Cannot — Help With

The RSS provides methodological and research design expertise. We are researchers, not consultants — our role is to strengthen the evidence base for your innovation, not to run your business or write your funding application.

Section 4: How to Work With Us — Your Journey with the RSS

Here is what to expect when you engage with the RSS, from first enquiry through to application support and beyond.

1
Get in touch. Contact us via NIHR's NHS Innovation Service or your regional RSS Hub. Acknowledgement within two working days. Tell us briefly about your company, technology, and support needs.
2
Quick eligibility check. We confirm Companies House registration, SME size criteria, and TRL3+. Usually one to two days. Have your Companies House number ready.
3
Scoping conversation. A 30–60 minute call to understand your innovation, research question, team, and needs. Completing the Innovator Engagement Form (Appendix B) in advance helps.
4
Agreed support plan. We confirm what we will provide, who leads it, and the timeframe — in writing.
5
Active research support. Study design workshops, health economics scoping, PPIE facilitation, application draft reviews, bid clinics, NHS infrastructure connections.
6
Signposting. Where needs fall outside our scope, we provide specific, contextualised signposting — with named contacts, not just links.
7
Staying connected. After submission, we stay in touch for future applications, wider NIHR connections, and potential case studies.

Section 6: Preparing for Your First Conversation — FAQs

You do not need to have everything figured out before you contact us — but the more you can tell us, the quicker we can identify the right support.

Useful things to think about first

  • Your company: registration, employee count, sector
  • Your innovation: what it does, what problem it solves, who it's for
  • Your evidence: what you've tested and what it shows
  • Your research question: the key question you want to answer
  • Your clinical connections: NHS partner or clinical champion
  • Your IP: owned by you, or involving a university
  • Your funding history: previous NIHR, Innovate UK, or SBRI funding
  • Your target funding: a specific call and its closing date
  • Your timeline: key milestones over the next 12–24 months

Frequently asked questions

We are very early stage — is it too soon to contact you?Never too early to have a conversation. If you are at TRL2 or below, we may not offer direct support yet — but we can point you toward getting to TRL3.
We do not have a clinical partner — does that matter?Most NIHR-funded research requires a clinical collaborator or NHS partner. We can help you think through what you need and potentially facilitate an introduction.
We are applying in six weeks — is it too late?Tight but not necessarily too late. Contact us immediately for an honest assessment. Engaging earlier always produces stronger applications.
We had a failed NIHR application before — can you still help?Absolutely. Understanding previous feedback and strengthening your next submission is exactly the kind of work we do.
We are worried about confidentiality.The RSS operates under strict data protection. We will not share your information without your explicit consent.
Do we need to apply for NIHR funding to work with you?No. There is no obligation to apply — our goal is to help you build the strongest possible evidence base.
We are a CIC or social enterprise — are we eligible?Yes, if registered as a limited company with Companies House and within SME size thresholds.
Our company is a university spinout — does anything change?No — spinouts are treated as any other SME. Your university's Technology Transfer Office may also be a useful contact.

Section 7: Understanding Your Research Stage — Technology Readiness Levels (TRL)

TRLs are a standard scale used by NIHR, Innovate UK, and most research funders to describe how mature a technology is — from TRL1 (basic concept) to TRL9 (proven in routine use).

TRL3 is the minimum entry point for NIHR RSS support and NIHR i4i funding — you must have at least some experimental or feasibility evidence, not just a concept or computer model.
TRLWhat it meansHealth & Care exampleWhat this means for NIHR access
TRL 1Basic scientific principles observedResearch shows a biomarker may indicate diseaseNot yet eligible — seek UKRI basic research funding or university collaboration
TRL 2Possible application identified, no data yetTheoretical breath sensor concept for cancer detectionNot yet eligible — consider Innovate UK feasibility grants, SetSquared, Northern Accelerator
TRL 3Proof of concept — lab/feasibility dataPrototype detects biomarker in controlled lab samplesNIHR RSS eligible — i4i (PDA, Connect, FAST), SBRI Healthcare
TRL 4Validated in a laboratory settingDevice validated on a larger sample set; safety profile startedRSS support — i4i PDA, FAST, SBRI Stage 1
TRL 5Validated in a near-clinical environmentTested in clinical lab with real patient samplesRSS support — evaluation design, NHS site planning, PPIE, stats
TRL 6Prototype demonstrated in a real operational settingPiloted in NHS outpatient clinic; early real-world dataRSS support — health economics, implementation planning, NICE ESF
TRL 7Near-final system tested at scaleMulti-site NHS trust evaluation completeRSS support — MHRA/UKCA planning, RDN engagement
TRL 8Technology completed and qualifiedUKCA marked; NICE evidence package submittedRSS limited scope — signpost to NICE, NHS Supply Chain, Health Innovation Networks
TRL 9Proven in routine operational useDevice in routine NHS use; post-market surveillanceRSS not typically relevant — signpost to NHS commercial teams and trade bodies

Seven questions to help you work out your TRL

QuestionIf YES, you are likely at...
Do you have documented experimental results or laboratory data?TRL 3+

Have you conducted a pilot study, feasibility trial, or prototype test?TRL 4+
Has your product been tested with real patients or service users?TRL 5+
Has it been tested or deployed in an NHS or equivalent healthcare setting?TRL 6+
Have you received any regulatory approvals (CE, UKCA, MHRA registration)?TRL 7+
Is your product currently in routine clinical use?TRL 8+

Appendix B: Innovator Engagement Form

When you first engage with the RSS, we may ask you to complete this form. It helps us understand your company, innovation, evidence base, and support needs. You can fill it in and send it in advance, or go through it together in your first conversation.

Please do not include any unprotected intellectual property in this form. Your information is handled in line with UK GDPR and will not be shared without your explicit consent. Learn more: nihr.ac.uk/research-support-service

Section 1: Your Company

  • Company name (as registered with Companies House)
  • Companies House number
  • Company type: Limited Company / CIC / Other
  • Number of employees
  • Annual turnover (approximate)
  • Company size: Micro / Small / Medium / Large
  • University spinout or start-up? (Yes/No — which university?)
  • Intellectual property: patent, trademark, copyright, licensed, under negotiation, none, other

Section 2: Previous Funding

  • Previous NIHR funding (stream, date, amount)
  • Previous Innovate UK funding (call, date, amount)

  • Other investment: VC, Angel/SEIS, Crowdfunding, Other

Section 3: Your Innovation

  • Plain-language description — what problem it solves, who it's for

  • Unique Selling Point (USP)

  • Stage: idea/concept, prototype, MVP, piloted, commercially available

  • Published data, evaluations, pilot reports, or case studies
